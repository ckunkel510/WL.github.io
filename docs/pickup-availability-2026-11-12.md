# Native pickup availability — November 12, 2026

The ordinary cart's pickup calendar allowed same-day requests based only on a two-hour preparation window. It did not compare requested quantities with the selected store or company inventory. The signed-in test cart contained 12 Turtlebox Ranger River Rock units (product 313359). The live labeled Available stock table showed Caldwell 2, Lexington 2, Groesbeck 3, Mexia 1, Buffalo 1, Brenham 0 and Bryan 0: 9 companywide. The candidate therefore allocates 2 local, 7 transferable and 3 requiring supplier ordering.

## Behavior

- Fetch the current signed-in native basket, not a cached shipping/analytics basket. Read quantities and product IDs, aggregate duplicate products before allocating inventory, and fetch labeled Available stock for all seven stores with three concurrent requests and bounded timeouts. Configured lines require confirmation of availability.
- Local quantities retain the two-hour preparation rule, hourly windows and Monday–Saturday schedule, using the America/Chicago calendar. Complete transfer pickups can be requested from two business days out; copy says transfers usually take 1–2 business days. Complete supplier pickups start two calendar weeks out, with a six-week request horizon and an explicit warning that availability and lead times require confirmation and can take longer. This is a conservative request floor, not a supplier ETA.
- Default to one complete pickup. When some quantity is local and some delayed, allow an explicit partial-pickup request. The first date then covers local quantities only. Native order instructions record per-product local, transfer, supplier and unconfirmed quantities and ask staff to arrange remaining pickups. This does not create multiple orders, shipments, reservations or appointments.
- Delayed requests require acknowledgement of estimates and ready-for-pickup confirmation. Missing inventory is unconfirmed, never treated as local or automatically classified as supplier stock. Its complete-pickup request uses the conservative two-week floor and requires store confirmation. An unreadable or signed-out basket blocks pickup continuation and offers recheck/back to cart.
- A deliberate Continue re-fetches basket and inventory. Changed quantities or allocations require review. Changed stores cannot accept late responses for the previous store; changing pickup plan clears acknowledgement and invalid early dates. Native validators also reject forged dates, Sundays, stale time windows and dates outside the current floor/horizon. Changes during the fulfillment confirmation cancel the pending continuation.
- Persist the requested native Required Date and full pickup instructions through the existing WebTrack path. Keep payment, totals, native Complete Order and UPS/local-delivery behavior unchanged. No new account storage, telemetry, third-party dependencies, credentials or remote service is introduced.

## Source and runtime

`PickupAvailability.js` owns parsing, allocation, scheduling and the pickup panel. `Checkout2.js` loads it only for ordinary native pickup, validates the result, and writes the existing native notes. `CartNativeViews.js` requests the new Checkout2 version. No WebTrack admin HTML change is needed. Ordinary checkout has no dependency on the compact Quick Buy runtime; Quick Buy retains its existing separate inventory policy (three business days for transfers and a minimum seven business days for supplier orders). The dormant February `wl-shoppingcart.bundle.js` contains an old wizard copy but is not requested by the current cart; the source/bundle relationship is documented in `WEBTRACK_FLOW_MAP.md`, and the dormant artifact is intentionally unchanged.

## Verification and limits

All 803 Node tests pass, including allocation, parsing, asset-loading and native-Continue regressions. Live inspection verified the prepared 12-unit basket, selected Caldwell store, same-day minimum and stock table. The native instructions textarea has no client maxlength. No order, payment, account edit or real cart quantity change was submitted. Candidate UI tests use the exact module with synthetic native basket/stock responses, including the observed Ranger counts. They do not mock or submit production payment requests. Native Continue integration tests exercise the actual proxy handler, validation/retry, repeated clicks, preflight failures and edits during confirmation. The review HTML offers all-local, transfer, supplier, mixed-product and unavailable-inventory scenarios. Browser tests verify allocation, acknowledgement, whole/partial requests, fresh checks, basket/store changes, late responses, failed inventory/baskets and forged dates. Layout checks at 390, 768 and 1280 pixels show no horizontal overflow and a 50px primary action. Console errors: zero.

This is browser-side expectation setting and validation, not an ERP reservation or a server-side oversell guarantee. Stock can change after the check. The store must confirm actual availability, supplier lead times and readiness; holiday closures and transfer routes are not present in the available stock response. A complete pickup request two weeks out can land on a holiday, so the displayed requested date remains subject to store confirmation. Before production release, the candidate should receive a live source-override pass through native details to review, stopping before Complete Order. Published asset availability and the actual native review instructions must then be checked after release.
